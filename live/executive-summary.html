1. What this project is

A read-only, browser-native dashboard for Kalshi's demo exchange covering five topics — gas prices, elections, culture, crypto and golf. It ranks events in the data it loads by a notional proxy, offers two alert feeds for observed moves of $0.10 or more, charts price and order-book liquidity, displays available resolutions, and runs four simulated strategies. Coverage and execution are limited as described below.

The site is static: HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step and no server. The browser attempts to fetch from the official Kalshi Trade API; an older, labelled snapshot is used if live fetching fails.

4. What the dashboard does

  • Ranking. Loaded events are ranked by volume × contract notional, a payout-notional proxy rather than actual traded dollars; pagination and missing values can affect ranking.
  • Alerts. Two chats: all moves ≥ $0.10, and the subset passing a documented reversion screen. Each message links to its market and its raw API call.
  • Charts. Official 1/60/1440-minute candlesticks merged with observed snapshots; window selectable from 15 minutes to 7 days.
  • Liquidity. Resting contracts at every returned price level, with the ask side correctly derived from NO bids.
  • Resolutions. Available YES/NO results are shown; capped discovery and the historical cutoff prevent a guarantee that every event is followed to resolution.
  • Simulated trading. Four strategies, fee-aware, auto-runnable, analysed by strategy, topic, side and price band.

5. Honest limitations

  • No cause data. Kalshi publishes prices and trades, not news. This site never asserts why a contract moved; the “what may have moved it” idea is handled on the flagged page because it cannot be sourced.
  • Demo subset. Several real series have no demo markets. Empty topics are reported as empty, never filled with examples.
  • Incomplete coverage and data gaps. Event discovery is capped, series pagination is not exhaustive, the live fallback is an older snapshot, and missing volume or notional can be defaulted in calculations. Rankings are not proven exhaustive or actual dollar turnover. See verification and audit limitations and historical cutoff.
  • Observed moves only. Polling compares snapshots and can miss swings that reverse between polls or while the page is closed; no continuous monitoring is guaranteed.
  • Simulated fills. The browser paper engine can mark a closed market as exited at the last observed price, not an executable quote; results are hypothetical. Official order book reference.
  • Polling, not a tape. The public page refreshes on a timer; true per-second streaming needs an authenticated WebSocket, which requires a key that is not in this repository.
  • No live account. The Kalshi demo-account balance needs signed API keys (F-06). The portfolio here is a local simulation.
  • No validated edge. The reversion screen and the four strategies are hypotheses; their results are published as-is.

6. What a trader should do with it

  1. Open the dashboard and check the freshness pill — green means data is under 2 minutes old.
  2. Use the topic pages to see one category at a time, events grouped inside each.
  3. Watch Alerts for ≥$0.10 moves; click any message to reach that market's chart and book.
  4. Review available spread, order-book size and volume; missing information may not be available for every row.
  5. Use paper trading only as an experimental simulation, not evidence of actual returns.
