Executive summary

What this repository is, what is verified, how the numbers are produced, and what is deliberately not claimed.

1. Purpose

A read-only monitoring and paper-trading desk for the Kalshi demo exchange, covering five topics only: gas prices, elections, culture, crypto and golf. It shows the highest-dollar live events, alerts on ≥ $0.10 contract moves in two scrolling chats (all events, and a rule-based high-ROI subset), charts price against time with seconds/minutes/hours axes, shows resting liquidity at each price, simulates trades, tracks their performance by strategy and topic, and records how events resolve (YES or NO).

4. Measured coverage

At the 2026-11-22 snapshot the demo host returned 18,298 open markets across these five filters (≈13.7k crypto, 2.1k gas, 1.1k golf, 0.95k culture, 0.44k elections), from the top 40 series by volume in each topic. The site publishes only markets that traded in 24h or carry open interest, capped per topic, and states how many were left out. Series counts at the same time: crypto 321, elections 2,710, entertainment 2,631, golf 134, oil & gas 25. Live counts are on the desk; raw counts are in data/meta.json.

5. Alerting and “high ROI”

  • Alert rule: |Δ last price| ≥ $0.10 between consecutive collection runs, in either direction. $0.10 on a $1.00-settling contract is 10 percentage points of implied probability.
  • Chat 1 shows every alert. Chat 2 shows alerts passing the screen: spread ≤ $0.03, ≥ 50 contracts at the ask, 24h volume ≥ 100 contracts, entry $0.05–$0.95. The screen is a transparent rule set, not a validated model.
  • Cause of a move is never asserted. The API does not report causes. Each alert lists the event's settlement sources as reference links only, explicitly marked unverified as causes.

6. Charts and liquidity

Price history comes from Kalshi's candlestick endpoint (1-minute and 1-hour candles) and the trade tape (individual prints with sub-second timestamps) for the most active markets; other markets fall back to run snapshots. Charts put time on X (seconds, minutes or hours) and YES price in dollars on Y, with recorded alerts marked. Liquidity comes from GET /markets/{ticker}/orderbook: both sides of the book, price by price, with resting contract counts and cumulative depth — i.e. how many contracts are available at a given price.

7. Trading, portfolio and strategies

Trades are simulated: 10 contracts, entered at the YES ask when a screened alert fires, bracketed ±$0.05 on the YES bid, with a 48-hour time stop, and settled at resolution. Performance is grouped by strategy (follow_rise, fade_drop, resolution hold), by topic and by alert direction, with win rate, realized P&L and average P&L per contract. Fees, slippage and queue position are not modelled, so results test the pipeline, not an edge. The real demo account (balance, positions, fills) is not connected because no API keys are stored in the repository.

8. What is verified, and what is not

Every endpoint and parameter on this site is listed with the official page that documents it on Sources. Every table row links to the API response that produced it. Values the API did not return are left blank and marked unverified. Items that could not be verified — including the absence of browser CORS support, the demo-account credential gap, and the fabricated content found in older pages of this repository — are on Flagged for review, and repository-level findings are on Repo review.

9. Known limits

  • Demo prices are simulated and may not match the production exchange.
  • Alert latency equals the collector cadence (GitHub Actions schedule, default every 10 minutes; GitHub documents that scheduled runs can be delayed).
  • Candles/tape cover the most active markets only.
  • Paper results have small samples early on; treat every P&L figure as a pipeline test.
  • The demo account is not connected, and no orders are ever sent.
